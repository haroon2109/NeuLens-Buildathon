# Wedge: NeuLens v1

**Status:** proposed focus (pre-pilot). This narrows NeuLens from a broad platform into a single job-to-be-done that can be validated, sold, and regulated.

## The wedge (one sentence)

**NeuLens is a browser-based, clinician-supervised cognitive screening and longitudinal change-monitoring workflow for memory clinics and primary-care practices** — a 10–15 minute, no-install session that captures structured cognitive, speech, and behavioral observations, compares each person to their own prior comparable screens, and packages an evidence-linked review record for the clinician (never a diagnosis).

## Initial ICP (ideal customer profile)

| | Who | Why they buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Beachhead buyer** | Outpatient memory clinics / geriatric & neurology practices running follow-up for MCI / early cognitive concerns | Need repeatable, low-burden follow-up between visits; want structured within-person change signals and documentation, not ad-hoc notes. |
| **Expansion buyer** | Primary-care groups doing cognitive screening/referral triage in older adults | Need a fast, guided screen that fits a roomed visit and produces a record a clinician can review and act on. |
| **Later / adjacent** | Senior-living wellness programs; pharma/CRO sites for trial pre-screening & longitudinal endpoints (requires validation) | Volume and longitudinal data, but higher evidence/integration bar. |

**Champion:** the clinician (neurologist, geriatrician, PCP, or neuropsych-trained nurse/PA) who reviews results. **Economic buyer:** practice/clinic lead or health-system innovation/primary-care ops. **User:** patient/caregiver completing a guided session on any modern browser.

## The job-to-be-done

> "When I’m following a patient with cognitive concerns, help me run a consistent, low-friction screen over time and tell me — with evidence and quality context — whether this person has meaningfully changed since *their own* baseline, so I can decide whether to reassure, repeat, or refer."

## Why this wedge (and not the whole platform)

- **Within-person longitudinal change** is the product’s native strength (baseline → comparable-session change detection → clinician review) and is more defensible/clinically sensible than a one-shot “risk score.”
- **Browser, no install, multilingual, low-literacy UX** lowers deployment friction for clinics and remote follow-up — a real distribution advantage if it holds up in pilots.
- **Clinician-in-the-loop, research-stage positioning** is the honest and regulatorily safer starting claim; it lets us generate evidence before making stronger claims.
- It avoids competing head-on with validated, reimbursed instruments on day one; it competes on *repeatable longitudinal workflow and evidence packaging*.

## What we explicitly do NOT claim (v1)

- Not a diagnosis, not a dementia/impairment classifier, not a disease-risk predictor.
- Not a validated clinical instrument and not a substitute for MoCA/MMSE/clinical evaluation (until/unless validated against them).
- Scores and “change” flags are exploratory review prompts; no validated reliable-change threshold or population norms in the MVP.
- Raw media is not the product; derived observations + clinician review are.

## Why now

- Aging population and rising MCI/dementia burden are pushing cognitive screening into primary care and follow-up workflows, but time and specialist access are constrained.
- Health systems need scalable, documentable between-visit monitoring; remote/browser delivery is now acceptable post-telehealth normalization.
- On-device/browser signal capture (vision/audio) is finally good enough to collect structured observations without dedicated hardware — if quality and validity are handled rigorously.

## 12‑month proof points (what “working” looks like)

1. **Design-partner pilots:** 3–5 clinics using NeuLens for real follow-up/triage workflows (documented). 
2. **Usability & completion:** patients (including low digital literacy / older adults, across offered languages) can complete a session with acceptable task quality and support burden.
3. **Clinical agreement signal:** within-person change flags and session summaries reviewed against clinician judgment and, where feasible, alongside an accepted instrument (e.g. MoCA) — directional evidence, IRB-approved where required.
4. **Workflow value:** clinicians report the review record saves time or clarifies follow-up decisions; at least one site moves from free pilot to paid/contracted.
5. **Safety/compliance readiness:** HIPAA-grade operating posture for pilots, auditability of records, and a documented validation/regulatory plan (see companion docs).
